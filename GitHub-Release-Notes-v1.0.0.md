# 1. Release Overview

Version **1.0.0** introduces the initial research framework of:

> **Portable Intelligence Runtime Pieces and Units — PIRP / PIRU**

The central question of this repository is:

> **Can intelligence itself become a portable, relayable, structurally localizable, composable, and computationally growable runtime object?**

The framework begins from **Per-Node Intelligence** and a simple observation.

An intelligent runtime may produce more than:

```text
1. Value / State
2. Structural Trigger
````

It may also produce a third kind of output:

```text
3. A bounded piece of runtime intelligence
   that can be preserved, carried,
   reused, published, relayed,
   composed, and grown.
```

This motivates:

* **PIRP — Portable Intelligence Runtime Piece**
* **PIRU — Portable Intelligence Runtime Unit**

and leads to a broader architecture for distributed intelligence growth.

---

# 2. PIRP

A **Portable Intelligence Runtime Piece (PIRP)** is a bounded-enough piece of intelligence that can potentially be:

```text
identified
carried
published
received
localized
continued
executed
observed
validated
composed
relayed
and grown
```

A PIRP is deliberately discovery-oriented.

Its useful intelligence may exist before humans have completely classified it as a:

```text
Function
State
Class
Agent
CallingGraph
CCC
DNA
Policy
Workflow
```

This leads to one of the central principles of the release:

> **Intelligence precedes its computational classification.**

---

# 3. PIRU

A **Portable Intelligence Runtime Unit (PIRU)** is a more formalized Portable Intelligence structure.

A PIRU normally has clearer:

```text
Identity
Boundary
Type
Interface
Runtime Contract
Validation
Policy
Version
```

The relationship is not:

```text
PIRP versus PIRU
```

but:

```text

        v
      PIRP
        |
        v

Boundary Discovery
Validation
Typing

        v
      PIRU
```

Runtime interaction can reopen a PIRU:

```text
PIRU

 v
Unexpected Structure
 |
 v
Structural Delta
 |
 v
New PIRP
```

Thus:

> **PIRP protects discovery; PIRU supports engineering.**

# 4. Pre-Categorical Intelligence

This release introduces the **Pre-Categorical Intelligence Hypothesis**:

> **Intelligence may exist as actionable, adaptive, composable, and growing runtime structure before it is classified into human-designed computational categories.**

Human computational abstractions remain extremely useful.

But they should not automatically be treated as the ontology of intelligence itself.

This motivates an intelligence-first engineering principle:

> **Preserve the runtime intelligence piece first; understand and unitize it second.**

---

# 5. Portable Across Intelligences

Traditional portability usually asks whether software can move across machines or runtimes.

PIRP/PIRU asks a broader question:

> **Can intelligence move across intelligences?**

The release introduces four levels:

```text
Computational Portability
        |
        v
Contextual Portability
        |
        v
Agent Portability
        |
        v
Civilizational Portability
```

A stronger Portability Test is therefore:

> **Can another intelligence receive this piece, localize it into its own Known-Knowledge, continue its computation, and grow from where the previous intelligence stopped?**

This produces a broader proposition:

> **A PIRP is not merely portable across runtimes; it may be portable across intelligences.**

One of the major extensions in v1.0.0 is the **Open PIRP**.

An Open PIRP is unresolved intelligence whose current frontier has nevertheless become sufficiently structured to be portable.

It may preserve:

```text

Context

Known
Unknown
Known-Unknown Boundary

Structural Gap

Evidence
Counter-Evidence

Constraints
Failed Attempts

Candidate Directions

Required Capabilities
Required Tools

Validation Conditions
Open Interfaces
```

The central proposition is:

> **A well-formed problem is executable intelligence whose next computation has not yet been completed.**

Therefore:

```text
Unresolved
!=
Unstructured
```

and:

```text
No Final Answer
!=
No Intelligence
```


Research may move this boundary without completely solving the problem.

Therefore:

> **Boundary refinement is itself intelligence growth.**

This gives Open PIRPs a meaningful lifecycle even when no final answer is reached.

# 11. Relay

Once intelligence is portable, another question appears:

> **Where should it go next?**

This release defines **Relay** as:

> **continued computation across an intelligence boundary.**

A relay is stronger than simple transfer.

```text
Transfer
=
Move Intelligence
```

while:

```text
Relay
=
Move Intelligence
+
Continue Computation
+
Attempt Structural Growth
```

A relay target may be:

```text
Human
LLM
Specialized Model
Algorithm
Search Engine
Database
Simulator
Formal Prover
PIRU
Team
Organization
Hybrid Human-AI Configuration
```

# 13. Provenance Is Not Enough

Traditional provenance asks:

> Where did this come from?

Relay Context additionally asks:

```text

What perspective was introduced?

What transformation occurred?

What Structural Delta followed?

Was the relay productive?
```

Thus:

> **Provenance tells us where intelligence came from; Relay Context tells us how it became productive somewhere else.**

PIRP/PIRU is not restricted to intelligence nodes.

Conceptually:


# 18. Relay Localization

Traditional Structural Localization asks:

> **Where is relevant intelligence?**

Relay Localization asks:

> **Who or what should continue this intelligence?**

The best relay may not be the closest social connection.

It may not even be an intelligence already known to the PIRP creator.

This leads to:

> **Social Proximity is not Intelligence Proximity.**

A useful relay may be:

> **organizationally distant but structurally near.**

---

# 19. Three Structural Distances

v1.0.0 introduces three useful distinctions.

### Knowledge Distance

How close is relevant existing intelligence?

### Capability Distance

How well does a candidate intelligence match the required continuation capability?

### Relay Distance

How difficult is productive transfer and continuation?

Relay Distance may include:

```text
Context Reconstruction
Terminology Gap
Representation Gap
Interface Gap
Tool Gap
Policy Constraints
Communication Cost
Validation Cost
```

The closest knowledge source is not necessarily the best continuation target.

---

# 20. Similarity Is Not Enough

A highly similar relay target may reproduce the same perspective.

A complementary intelligence may create more useful growth.

```text
High Similarity
!=
High Growth Potential
```

Relay Localization therefore introduces the problem of **Structural Complementarity**.

The desired target may combine:

```text
Relevant Knowledge
+
Complementary Capability
+
Useful Perspective
+
Available Tools
+
Acceptable Relay Distance
```

> **Computational Growth Localization**

This changes localization from retrieval alone into a growth operation.

# 34. v1.0.0 Design Boundary

This first release intentionally establishes the **conceptual and structural framework before fixing an implementation architecture**.

It does not prescribe a mandatory:

```text
Java Runtime
Agent Framework
Central Registry
Database
Network Protocol
Serialization Format
Universal PIRP Schema
Global Capability Ranking
```

This is deliberate.

The repository first asks:

```text
What is Portable Intelligence?

How should emerging runtime intelligence be preserved?

What distinguishes PIRP from PIRU?

Can unresolved problems themselves become Portable Intelligence?

How does intelligence cross human and AI boundaries?

What should be preserved when intelligence is relayed?

How can we locate the next intelligence capable of continuing it?

How can relay history improve future relay?

How can these structures support Computational Growth?
```

Engineering implementations can follow from these questions without prematurely narrowing the research space.

# 37. Release Perspective

PIRP/PIRU begins with a small conceptual shift:

> Instead of asking only what an intelligent system computes, ask what pieces of intelligence its runtime produces that could survive beyond the immediate computation.

Once such a piece becomes explicit, a much larger chain becomes visible:

```text
Runtime Intelligence
        |
        v
Portable Intelligence
        |
        v
Published Intelligence
        |
        v
Discoverable Intelligence
        |
        v
Localized Intelligence
        |
        v
Relayed Intelligence
        |
        v
Continued Intelligence
        |
        v
Structural Delta
        |
        v
Collective Learning
        |
        v
Computational Growth
```

This suggests a different way to view distributed intelligence.

The important transferable object may not always be the entire model, agent, application, organization, or runtime.

Sometimes it may be:

> **the intelligence piece itself.**

And once intelligence pieces can be explicitly published, discovered, localized, relayed, composed, and grown, the Internet's existing research and engineering infrastructure may begin to function as something richer than an information network.

It may progressively become:

> **a distributed runtime for the continued growth of intelligence itself.**
