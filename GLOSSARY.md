# GLOSSARY

## Portable Intelligence Runtime Pieces and Units — PIRP / PIRU

### Canonical Terminology for Portable, Relayable, and Computationally Growing Intelligence

---

## Purpose

This glossary defines the canonical terminology used throughout the PIRP/PIRU repository.

The definitions are intentionally structural rather than implementation-specific.

PIRP/PIRU is not proposed as a fixed software object model. The terminology is intended to support reasoning about intelligence that can be:

```text
recognized
bounded
carried
published
localized
continued
executed
validated
composed
relayed
and grown
````

---

# A

## Agent

Any intelligence-bearing or computation-capable participant that can receive, transform, validate, execute, or relay a PIRP/PIRU.

Examples may include:

* Human
* LLM
* Specialized Model
* Algorithm
* Tool
* Simulator
* Database
* PIRU
* Team
* Organization
* Hybrid System

In this repository, **Agent** is a useful perspective, but not the fundamental ontology of Portable Intelligence.

A PIRP does not have to be an Agent.

---

## Agent Portability

The ability of an intelligence structure to move from one intelligence-bearing agent to another while preserving enough meaning and operational structure for useful continuation.

Examples:

```text
Human -> Human
Human -> AI
AI    -> Human
AI    -> AI
```

Agent Portability is broader than software portability.

See also:

* Portable Intelligence
* Contextual Portability
* Civilizational Portability

---

## AI PIRP Extractor

An AI mechanism that recognizes candidate PIRPs from existing artifacts.

Possible sources include:

```text
Paper
Repository
Issue
Question
Experiment
Benchmark
Research Note
DOI
Code
Dataset
```

Its task is not merely summarization.

It attempts to recover machine-operable structures such as:

```text
Problem
Context
Known
Unknown
Evidence
Counter-Evidence
Open Ends
Required Capabilities
Lineage
```

---

## AI Open-PIRP Generator

An AI mechanism that identifies an unresolved structural gap and constructs an Open PIRP representing the current intelligence frontier.

Conceptually:

```text
Observation
    |
    v
Gap Detection
    |
    v
Existing-Knowledge Search
    |
    v
No Sufficient Resolution
    |
    v
Open PIRP Construction
```

The important premise is:

> **Detecting and structuring a meaningful gap can itself be intelligence.**

---

# B

## Boundary

The operational distinction between what belongs to a PIRP/PIRU and what remains external to it.

A boundary may involve:

* state,
* context,
* computation,
* evidence,
* policy,
* interfaces,
* dependencies,
* ownership,
* runtime scope.

A PIRP may have an emerging or partially understood boundary.

A PIRU generally requires a more explicit boundary.

---

## Boundary Discovery

The process of identifying the effective limits of an emerging PIRP.

Boundary Discovery may involve:

```text
Observation
Comparison
Counter-Evidence
Runtime Testing
Interface Detection
Dependency Analysis
Structural Differentiation
```

It is an important step in:

```text
PIRP -> PIRU
```

---

## Bridge PIRP

A PIRP that connects two intelligence structures, domains, perspectives, representations, or computational regions that were previously weakly connected.

A Bridge PIRP may capture:

* analogy,
* mapping,
* translation,
* shared structure,
* interface,
* transformation,
* cross-domain insight.

Bridge PIRPs can be important sources of Structural Growth.

---

# C

## CallingGraph — CG

A graph describing calling, dependency, continuation, or structural relationships among computational or intelligence objects.

Within the PIRP/PIRU framework, CallingGraph is generalized beyond conventional software function calls.

Important forms include:

* Knowledge CallingGraph
* Relay CallingGraph
* Collective Intelligence CallingGraph

---

## Capability

The ability of an intelligence-bearing entity to perform a class of useful computation or structural transformation.

Examples:

```text
Mathematical Proof
Code Generation
Simulation
Counter-Evidence Search
Domain Interpretation
Experimental Validation
Structural Matching
Policy Analysis
```

Capability is contextual.

An agent may be highly capable for one PIRP and poorly matched for another.

---

## Capability Distance

A measure or structural estimate of how closely an agent's capabilities match the capabilities required by a PIRP.

Conceptually:

```text
PIRP Required Capability
          |
          v
Capability Matching
          |
          v
Candidate Agent
```

Capability Distance should not be interpreted as a permanent ranking of agents.

It is relational and task-dependent.

See also:

* Structural Comparative Advantage
* Relay Localization
* Relay Distance

---

## Capability Graph

A structural representation connecting:

```text
Agents
Capabilities
Contexts
Tools
PIRP Types
Relay Outcomes
```

A Capability Graph can support Relay Localization.

It differs from a Social Graph because it focuses on structural qualification rather than acquaintance or organizational proximity.

---

## Candidate PIRP

A PIRP representing a plausible but not yet sufficiently validated intelligence structure.

It may contain:

* hypothesis,
* candidate mechanism,
* tentative relation,
* proposed algorithm,
* possible explanation,
* preliminary structural pattern.

A Candidate PIRP should preserve its validation status.

---

## CCC

A reusable structural intelligence representation used elsewhere in the Structural Intelligence framework.

Within PIRP/PIRU:

> **CCC can encode reusable folded structure; PIRP/PIRU describes a broader portable runtime intelligence object.**

CCC may be contained in, referenced by, generated from, or used to construct a PIRP/PIRU.

CCC and PIRP are therefore related but not interchangeable.

---

## Civilizational Portability

The ability of intelligence to survive transfer across long periods, institutions, communities, technologies, or generations.

Examples include intelligence preserved through:

```text
Papers
Books
Repositories
DOIs
Standards
Algorithms
Experimental Records
Structured Open Problems
```

Civilizational Portability is the broadest portability level discussed in this repository.

---

## Collective Intelligence

Intelligence produced through the interaction of multiple humans, AIs, tools, algorithms, organizations, and reusable intelligence structures.

In PIRP/PIRU, Collective Intelligence includes not only shared knowledge but also:

```text
Open Problems
Counter-Evidence
Failed Attempts
Relay History
Capability Relations
Structural Deltas
Growth Trajectories
```

---

## Collective Intelligence CallingGraph

A structural graph combining knowledge relationships with the actual history of intelligence transfer and growth.

Conceptually:

```text
Knowledge CallingGraph
        +
Relay CallingGraph
        +
Context
        +
Capability Evidence
        +
Structural Delta
        +
Outcome
        |
        v
Collective Intelligence CallingGraph
```

A canonical relay trajectory may be represented as:

```text
Intelligence-A
      |
      v
Agent-B
      |
      v
Context-C
      |
      v
Perspective-P
      |
      v
Structural-Delta-D
      |
      v

```

---

## Collective Intelligence History

The preserved structural history of:

```text
what intelligence existed,
who or what received it,
under what context,
through which perspective,
what changed,
what failed,
what succeeded,
and what remained open.
```

This is richer than ordinary provenance or citation history.

---

## Collective Learning

The process through which a distributed intelligence ecology improves by accumulating both:

```text
Knowledge Growth
+
Relay Intelligence Growth
```

Collective Learning therefore includes learning:

1. what intelligence exists;
2. how intelligence can be productively transferred, localized, continued, and grown.

A central principle is:

> **Collective Learning does not only make the Knowledge CallingGraph larger; it can make the system increasingly good at relaying.**

---

## Composability

The ability of a PIRP/PIRU to participate in a larger intelligence structure.

Composition may involve:

```text
PIRP + PIRP
PIRP + PIRU
PIRU + PIRU
PIRP + Tool
PIRP + Policy
PIRP + Agent
```

Composition does not require that all participants use the same internal representation.

---

## Composite PIRP

A PIRP constructed from multiple intelligence pieces while preserving a meaningful higher-level identity.

Example:

```text
PIRP-A
+
PIRP-B
+
PIRU-C
+
Policy
    |
    v
Composite PIRP
```

A Composite PIRP may itself become a candidate PIRU.

---

## Computational Growth

Growth in the structure or capability of an intelligence system through meaningful Structural Deltas.

Computational Growth may include:

* new PIRPs,
* new PIRUs,
* specialization,
* composition,
* new relations,
* new evidence,
* new interfaces,
* new capabilities,
* new relay structures,
* new intelligence types.

Computational Growth is not equivalent to merely increasing object count.

---

## Computational Growth Intelligence

An intelligence capability concerned not only with solving tasks, but with recognizing, producing, localizing, validating, composing, and growing intelligence structures themselves.

A canonical loop is:

```text
Observe
   |
   v
Detect Intelligence / Gap
   |
   v
PIRP
   |
   v
Search / Localize
   |
   v
Execute / Relay
   |
   v
Structural Delta
   |
   v
New PIRP
```

---

## Computational Growth Localization

The generalized localization problem of finding the combination of intelligence resources most likely to enable productive Structural Growth.

Conceptually:

```text
Given PIRP P,

find:

Knowledge K
Agent A
Tool T
Context C
Policy G

such that useful Structural Growth
becomes possible.
```

This extends localization beyond retrieval.

---

## Computational Portability

The ability of a PIRP/PIRU to operate across computational environments.

Examples include portability across:

* machines,
* operating environments,
* runtimes,
* execution nodes,
* compatible computational infrastructures.

It is the narrowest portability level in this framework.

---

## Context

The local conditions required to correctly interpret, execute, continue, validate, or relay intelligence.

Context may include:

```text
State
Environment
History
Assumptions
Constraints
Perspective
Policy
Available Tools
Known-Knowledge
Runtime Conditions
```

Portable Intelligence does not require eliminating context.

It requires preserving enough context for meaningful continuation.

---

## Contextual Portability

The ability of intelligence to survive movement into a different context without losing the structure necessary for useful interpretation or continuation.

This often requires preservation of Minimum Sufficient Portable Context.

---

## Continuation Cost

The amount of work required for a receiving intelligence to reconstruct enough structure and context to continue a PIRP productively.

A high-quality Portable Intelligence artifact attempts to reduce unnecessary Continuation Cost.

Conceptually:

```text
Poorly Structured Publication
        |
        v
High Reconstruction Cost


Well-Structured PIRP
        |
        v
Lower Continuation Cost
```

---

## Continuation Interface

The part of an Open PIRP that exposes where another intelligence can productively continue computation.

Possible fields include:

```text
Current Boundary
Required Capability
Useful Perspectives
Failed Perspectives
Needed Evidence
Required Tools
Validation Conditions
Open Ends
```

A Continuation Interface differs from a conventional execution interface.

Its purpose is not merely to invoke existing behavior.

Its purpose is to expose where new intelligence can enter.

---

## Counter-Evidence

Evidence that challenges, limits, contradicts, or reduces confidence in a candidate intelligence structure.

Counter-Evidence is treated as a first-class structural object rather than unwanted noise.

---

## Counter-Evidence PIRP

A portable intelligence piece whose primary contribution is evidence against an existing hypothesis, structure, PIRP, PIRU, or relation.

A Counter-Evidence PIRP may:

* invalidate a candidate,
* restrict a boundary,
* expose a hidden condition,
* create a new branch,
* reopen a PIRU,
* trigger new Structural Growth.

---

# D

## Dispatch

The operational act of directing a PIRP toward a selected knowledge resource, agent, tool, PIRU, team, or hybrid configuration.

Dispatch follows Localization.

Conceptually:

```text
PIRP
 |
 v
Localization
 |
 v
Candidate
 |
 v
Policy Check
 |
 v
Dispatch
```

---

## DNA

A compact structural encoding or dispatch-oriented representation used in the broader Structural Intelligence framework.

Within PIRP/PIRU:

> **DNA is a possible structural encoding inside the intelligence system; PIRP/PIRU is the portable runtime intelligence object that may use such encodings.**

DNA and PIRP/PIRU therefore occupy different abstraction roles.

---

# E

## Edge Intelligence

Intelligence encoded in the relationship between intelligence objects rather than only in the objects themselves.

For example:

```text
PIRP-A
   |
   | under Context-C
   | received by Agent-B
   | through Perspective-P
   | producing Delta-D
   v
PIRP-E
```

The edge may preserve enough reusable intelligence to become a PIRP itself.

This leads to:

```text
Node     = PIRP
Edge     = PIRP
Subgraph = PIRP
Graph    = PIRP
```

---

## Emergence

The appearance of meaningful runtime intelligence before its final category, boundary, type, or interface is fully understood.

Emergence motivates PIRP as a discovery-oriented abstraction.

---

## Evolvability

The ability of a PIRP/PIRU to participate in controlled structural change.

Possible mechanisms include:

```text
Specialization
Branching
Composition
Replacement
Interface Growth
Evidence Growth
Type Growth
Relay Growth
```

Evolvability does not imply unrestricted mutation.

---

## Expected Structural Growth

A conceptual estimate of the useful Structural Delta that may result from a particular localization or relay.

It may depend on:

```text
PIRP
Knowledge
Agent
Capability
Tool
Context
Policy
Relay History
```

It is a decision concept, not necessarily a single scalar score.

---

# F

## Failed-Attempt PIRP

A PIRP preserving a meaningful unsuccessful attempt.

A useful Failed-Attempt PIRP may contain:

```text
Problem
Context
Attempt
Expected Outcome
Observed Outcome
Failure
Evidence
Interpretation
Updated Boundary
```

Its value is that future intelligences can avoid repeating the same search path or can reinterpret the failure under a different context.

> **A validated failure can be positive intelligence.**

---

## Folding

The process of compressing, organizing, or encoding repeated or reusable structural experience into a more reusable representation.

Within PIRP/PIRU, Folding may transform runtime experience into structures such as CCC or DNA.

Folding is distinct from Unitization.

* **Unitization** determines what bounded intelligence object exists.
* **Folding** determines how structural experience can be compactly represented or organized.

---

# G

## Growth Commons

A shared intelligence space containing not only established knowledge but also explicit opportunities for future Structural Growth.

It may expose:

```text
Unknowns
Open PIRPs
Failed Attempts
Counter-Evidence
Validation Needs
Missing Capabilities
Open Interfaces
Unresolved Branches
```

The Growth Commons extends the traditional Knowledge Commons.

---

## Growth Hook

An explicit interface through which a PIRP/PIRU may be extended, specialized, composed, challenged, replaced, or reopened.

Growth Hooks help make evolvability operational.

---

## Growth PIRP

A PIRP whose primary function is to represent or enable a Structural Growth opportunity.

Examples may include:

* new branch,
* extension proposal,
* interface expansion,
* cross-domain bridge,
* candidate specialization,
* structural replacement.

---

# I

## Identifiable

The property that a PIRP/PIRU can be distinguished sufficiently from surrounding intelligence for reference, transfer, observation, or lineage tracking.

Identifiability does not require perfect ontological boundaries.

---

## Intelligence Boundary

Examples:

```text
Human -> Human
Human -> AI
AI -> Human
AI -> AI
Team -> Tool
PIRU -> Agent
Organization -> Organization
Generation -> Generation
```

Relay occurs across an Intelligence Boundary.

## Intelligence Commons

Possible substrate:

DOIs

## Intelligence Ecology

See **Portable Intelligence**.

## Intelligence Proximity

Intelligence Proximity may depend on:

# K

## Knowledge CallingGraph

A graph representing structural relationships among intelligence objects.

It answers:

> **What intelligence extends, depends on, contradicts, validates, specializes, implements, or composes with what intelligence?**

Possible edges include:

```text
extends
depends-on
contradicts
validates
specializes
generalizes
implements
composes-with
resolves
opens-question
derived-from
```

The Knowledge CallingGraph describes structural knowledge relations.

It does not by itself describe how intelligence actually moved among agents.

See:

* Relay CallingGraph
* Collective Intelligence CallingGraph

---

## Knowledge Distance

A structural estimate of how far a PIRP is from existing relevant knowledge.

Knowledge Distance may be based on:

* structural similarity,
* semantic similarity,
* shared context,
* shared dependencies,
* graph position,
* evidence relations,
* metric-space representations.

---

## Known

The portion of an intelligence frontier currently treated as sufficiently established for the present context.

Known does not necessarily mean universally true.

It is contextual and may later be challenged.

---

## Known-Knowledge

The structured knowledge currently available to an intelligence-bearing system.

Receiving a PIRP may modify Known-Knowledge through:

```text
Addition
Reinterpretation
Contradiction
Specialization
Composition
Validation
Rejection
```

---

## Known-Unknown Boundary

The current structural frontier separating what is sufficiently understood from what remains unresolved.

A major Open-PIRP principle is:

> **Movement of the Known-Unknown Boundary can itself represent intelligence growth.**

---

# L

## Leftover

Structure that does not yet fit the dominant formalization, cluster, type, branch, or model.

A Leftover may represent:

* noise,
* insufficient evidence,
* an exception,
* a new category,
* an emerging PIRP,
* a future Growth Hook.

Therefore Leftovers should not automatically be discarded.

---

## Lineage

The structural history of how a PIRP/PIRU was derived, transformed, specialized, composed, validated, or replaced.

Possible lineage relations include:

```text
derived-from
specializes
generalizes
contradicts
validates
implements
composes-with
replaces
opens-question
```

Lineage is part of Portable Intelligence because it reduces reconstruction cost and supports Collective Learning.

---

## Localization

The process of finding a structurally appropriate target for an intelligence object.

PIRP/PIRU distinguishes:

* Structural Localization
* Relay Localization
* Computational Growth Localization

---

# M

## Minimum Sufficient Portable Context

The smallest practical context package that allows another intelligence to correctly interpret, localize, continue, validate, or execute a PIRP.

The objective is not:

```text
Remove All Context
```

but:

```text
Preserve Enough Context
for Productive Continuation
```

This is central to Contextual Portability.

# O

## Open End

An explicitly preserved location where additional intelligence may enter.

Examples:

```text
Unresolved Question
Missing Evidence
Unexplored Branch
Unknown Boundary
Missing Capability
Candidate Interface
Unvalidated Hypothesis
```

An Open End can become a Growth Hook or Open PIRP.

---

## Open Interface

An explicit continuation point exposed by an unresolved intelligence structure.

Open Interfaces make an Open PIRP easier to relay.

---

## Open PIRP

An unresolved but sufficiently structured Portable Intelligence Runtime Piece that preserves the current intelligence frontier and exposes interfaces for continued computation.

A canonical Open PIRP may contain:

```text
Identity
Problem
Context

Known
Unknown
Known-Unknown Boundary

Structural Gap

Evidence
Counter-Evidence

Constraints
Failed Attempts

Candidate Directions

Required Capabilities
Required Tools

Validation Conditions
Open Interfaces

Lineage
Status
```

Central principle:

> **A well-formed problem is executable intelligence whose next computation has not yet been completed.**

An Open PIRP is not necessarily incomplete intelligence.

It is intelligence with an unresolved continuation frontier.

---

## Operationally Actionable

The property that a PIRP contains enough structure for some meaningful next operation.

That operation may be:

```text
Execute
Search
Validate
Relay
Investigate
Compare
Compose
Challenge
Extend
```

Therefore operational actionability is broader than machine-code executability.

## Portable Intelligence

Intelligence preserved in a form that can survive meaningful transfer across runtime, context, agent, organization, or generation boundaries.

A strong portability test is:

> **Can another intelligence receive this piece, localize it into its own Known-Knowledge, continue its computation, and grow from where the previous intelligence stopped?**

Portable Intelligence may exist as PIRP or PIRU.

## Productive Continuation

A continuation event that produces a useful Structural Delta.

Relay success is therefore not merely:

```text
PIRP Delivered
```

but:

```text
PIRP Received
      |
      v
Understood / Localized
      |
      v
Continued
      |
      v
Useful Structural Delta
```

## Provenance

Information describing where an intelligence artifact came from.

Examples:

```text
Author
Source
Repository
Parent PIRP
Version
Timestamp
Derivation
```

Provenance is important but narrower than Relay Context.

> **Provenance tells us where intelligence came from; Relay Context tells us how it became productive somewhere else.**

---

## Publish

The operation of externalizing a PIRP/PIRU into a larger intelligence environment so that another intelligence may discover, localize, validate, continue, or grow it.

Conceptually:

```text
publish(PIRP)
```

In this framework:

> **Publish is not merely an information-distribution operation. It can be an intelligence-transfer operation.**

Context and capability matter.

## Relay

The transfer and continuation of computation across an Intelligence Boundary.

A canonical relay is:

```text

        v

        v

        |
        v
Structural Delta

Canonical principle:

> **Relay is continued computation across an intelligence boundary.**

Relay is stronger than forwarding or delivery.

A graph describing how intelligence actually moved among humans, AIs, tools, PIRUs, teams, and other intelligence-bearing entities.

It answers:

> **How did intelligence move, who or what received it, under what context, and what Structural Growth followed?**

Relay CallingGraph edges may preserve:

## Relay Distance

The difficulty of transferring a PIRP to a particular receiver in a form that permits Productive Continuation.

Relay Distance may depend on:

Capability Gap

Reconstruction Cost

A receiver can be close in Knowledge Distance but far in Relay Distance.

A concrete instance in which a PIRP crosses an Intelligence Boundary and is processed by another intelligence.

   v

A relay that does not produce the expected Productive Continuation.

Missing Tool
Policy Block

No Useful Structural Delta

Relay Failure should be preserved because it can improve future localization.

## Relay History

The accumulated sequence of Relay Events associated with a PIRP, agent, domain, capability, or intelligence lineage.

Relay History can become structural training material for future dispatch.

## Relay Intelligence

Intelligence about how Portable Intelligence can be successfully transferred and continued.

It may include knowledge of:

Which Agents
Which Contexts
Which Perspectives
Which Tools
Which Relay Patterns
```

tend to produce useful Structural Deltas.

## Relay Interface

Expected Structural Delta

## Relay Localization

The process of finding the human, AI, tool, PIRU, team, organization, or hybrid intelligence best positioned to continue a PIRP.

It answers:

> **Who or what should continue this intelligence?**

Relay Localization is distinct from Structural Localization.

## Solved PIRP

A PIRP whose primary problem or open frontier has reached a sufficiently validated resolution for the present context.

Solved does not mean permanently closed.

## Structural Comparative Advantage

        |
        v

Structural Comparative Advantage is:

* contextual,
* empirical,
* revisable,
* relation-specific.

It is not intended as a permanent global ranking of humans or AIs.

Structural Growth is a major form of Computational Growth.

## Structural Intelligence

## Trigger

A structural condition or mechanism that determines what computation, behavior, or transition should occur next.

# Core Principles

### Principle 1

> **Intelligence precedes its computational classification.**

### Principle 2

> **Preserve emerging intelligence before forcing complete formalization.**

### Principle 3

> **PIRP protects discovery; PIRU supports engineering.**

### Principle 4

> **Piece means bounded intelligence identity, not necessarily smallness.**

### Principle 5

> **A PIRP may be portable across intelligences, not only across runtimes.**

### Principle 6

> **A well-formed problem can itself be executable intelligence.**

### Principle 7

> **A validated failure can be positive intelligence.**

### Principle 8

> **Relay is continued computation across an intelligence boundary.**

### Principle 9

> **The Relay Event is itself an intelligence object.**

### Principle 10

> **Provenance tells us where intelligence came from; Relay Context tells us how it became productive somewhere else.**

### Principle 11

> **Social Proximity is not Intelligence Proximity.**

### Principle 12

> **Localization can search not only for where an answer exists, but for where intelligence can productively continue growing.**

### Principle 13

> **Capability and authority should remain distinguishable.**

### Principle 14

> **Every relay can teach the system how to relay better next time.**

### Principle 15

> **Collective Learning includes both Knowledge Growth and Relay Intelligence Growth.**

### Principle 16

> **Open Ends, Leftovers, Counter-Evidence, and Failed Attempts can all become future Growth Interfaces.**

### Principle 17

> **The path of intelligence growth is itself intelligence.**

### Principle 18

> **No participant needs to join the whole intelligence system. Each participant only needs to pick up one PIRP.**
