# PIRP-PIRU-002 — Pre-Categorical Intelligence and the PIRP/PIRU Duality

## Abstract

Modern computing describes intelligent behavior through categories such as state, function, class, rule, model, agent, graph, node, memory, policy, and service.

These categories are indispensable engineering tools.

But they may not be ontologically primary.

Natural and biological intelligence did not emerge by first defining functions, classes, agents, states, or graph nodes. Adaptive behavior, memory, response, specialization, composition, and growth existed before human observers classified them into convenient conceptual and computational categories.

This article introduces the **Pre-Categorical Intelligence Hypothesis**:

> **Intelligence may exist as actionable, adaptive, composable, and growing structure before it is classified into human-designed computational categories.**

From this perspective, the distinction between **PIRP — Portable Intelligence Runtime Piece** and **PIRU — Portable Intelligence Runtime Unit** becomes fundamental.

PIRP emphasizes the preservation of intelligence as encountered, discovered, or emerging in runtime reality.

PIRU emphasizes the later act of bounding, typing, validating, interfacing, and engineering that intelligence into a reusable computational unit.

The relationship is therefore not PIRP versus PIRU.

It is a recurring intelligence lifecycle:

> **PIRP -> recognition -> PIRU -> execution -> interaction -> growth -> new PIRP**

This duality provides a bridge between open-ended intelligence growth and disciplined computational engineering.

> **A useful computational classification is not necessarily the same thing as the intelligence being classified.**

This distinction becomes increasingly important as AI systems move from predefined computation toward structural discovery and computational growth.

Before humans created concepts such as:

* function,
* state,
* object,

* graph,
* rule,

```text

    |

    |
    v

    v
```

The categories came later.

Observers recognized recurring structures and gave them names.

Engineers subsequently turned many of those conceptual distinctions into explicit computational abstractions.

This suggests a simple but important principle:

> **Intelligence precedes its computational classification.**

The statement does not imply that every natural process is intelligent.

Nor does it imply that computational categories are artificial in the sense of being useless.

It means something more specific:

> The behavior or structure we wish to understand may exist before we know which of our existing categories best describes it.

## 3. The Pre-Categorical Intelligence Hypothesis

We therefore propose the following working hypothesis:

> **Pre-Categorical Intelligence Hypothesis:**
> Intelligence may exist as executable, actionable, adaptive, composable, context-sensitive, or growing structure before it is classified into human-designed computational categories.

The term **pre-categorical** does not mean structureless.

On the contrary, such intelligence may contain rich structure.

What is missing is not necessarily structure.

What may be missing is a satisfactory human classification of that structure.

For example, an emerging runtime intelligence piece may contain:

```text
local state
+
trigger behavior
+
structural knowledge
+
context
+
memory
+
policy
+
partial execution logic
+
growth potential
```

The engineering temptation is to immediately ask:

> Is this a function?

> Is this an object?

> Is this an agent?

> Is this a CCC?

> Is this a CallingGraph node?

But these questions may be premature.

The first question should instead be:

> **What intelligence exists here?**

Only afterward should we ask:

> **How should we classify, bound, represent, and engineer it?**

## 4. Perspective Is Not Ontology

Structural Intelligence repeatedly encounters the importance of perspective.

The same runtime phenomenon can often be observed through multiple structural views.

For example:

```text
Runtime Intelligence
        |
        +----> State Perspective
        |
        +----> Function Perspective
        |
        +----> CallingGraph Perspective
        |
        +----> CCC Perspective
        |
        +----> DNA Perspective
        |
        +----> Policy Perspective
        |
        +----> Memory Perspective
```

Each perspective may reveal something important.

None must necessarily be the complete ontology of the intelligence itself.

This distinction can be written conceptually as:

```text
Intelligence
     |
     v
Observation
     |
     +----> Perspective A
     |
     +----> Perspective B
     |
     +----> Perspective C
```

The perspectives are projections.

The intelligence being projected may be richer than any one of them.

This is one reason PIRP is useful.

PIRP allows us to say:

> **There is a meaningful runtime intelligence piece here even if we have not yet decided which existing perspective should dominate its representation.**

## 5. Why PIRP Uses "Piece"

The word **Piece** intentionally avoids excessive formal commitment.

A PIRP may be:

* partially understood,
* irregular,
* heterogeneous,
* incompletely bounded,
* context-sensitive,
* structurally novel,
* still growing,
* or difficult to classify.

The word does not assert that the intelligence has already been normalized into a standard engineering object.

This gives PIRP an intentionally permissive character.

Conceptually:

```text
Something intelligent is happening here.
              |
              v
       Preserve the piece.
              |
              v
      Observe and understand.
              |
              v
      Formalize only when ready.
```

This can be called a **discovery-first** posture.

PIRP therefore protects against a common failure mode:

> **Premature Formalization**

Premature formalization occurs when a newly emerging intelligence structure is forced into an existing abstraction before its actual structure is sufficiently understood.

The result may be loss of:

* context,
* structural relations,
* alternative interpretations,
* growth potential,
* hidden state,
* counter-evidence,
* or unexpected interfaces.

PIRP provides room for the structure to remain partially open.

---

## 6. Why PIRU Uses "Unit"

The word **Unit** serves a different purpose.

Engineering eventually requires stronger commitments.

A reusable runtime intelligence object may need:

```text
identity
boundary
type
interface
runtime contract
input semantics
output semantics
state semantics
validation
governance
versioning
composition rules
```

At this point, the intelligence is no longer merely being preserved.

It is being **unitized**.

We therefore define PIRU as the formalization-oriented counterpart of PIRP.

Conceptually:

```text
PIRP
 |
 | observe
 | identify invariants
 | discover boundary
 | extract interface
 | validate behavior
 | define lifecycle
 v
PIRU
```

PIRU is therefore a **formalization-first engineering object**, but only after enough discovery has occurred.

---

## 7. PIRP and PIRU Are Not Competing Names

It would be easy to treat PIRP and PIRU as two candidate acronyms and ask which one should win.

That framing is unnecessarily restrictive.

A more useful interpretation is:

> **PIRP and PIRU represent two complementary relationships to intelligence.**

PIRP asks:

> What intelligence appears to exist here?

PIRU asks:

> How can this intelligence be bounded and engineered?

The contrast can be summarized as follows:

| Dimension       | PIRP                   | PIRU                       |
| --------------- | ---------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Primary posture | discovery              | formalization              |
| Boundary        | may be emerging        | explicit                   |
| Structure       | heterogeneous          | normalized                 |
| Typing          | optional or incomplete | defined                    |
| Identity        | may emerge through use | declared                   |
| Interface       | may be implicit        | explicit                   |
| Growth          | open-ended             | controlled                 |
| Validation      | exploratory            | systematic                 |
| Composition     | opportunistic          | governed                   |
| Primary concern | preserve intelligence  | make intelligence reusable |

Neither is sufficient alone.

## 8. Liberal Discovery and Formal Engineering

The PIRP/PIRU distinction can also be understood as a tension between two legitimate design philosophies.

### PIRP: Liberal Discovery

PIRP gives emerging intelligence the benefit of structural freedom.

It allows:

```text
unusual combinations
partial structures
open boundaries
temporary identities
experimental interfaces
unexpected growth
cross-category behavior
```

Its bias is:

> **Do not discard intelligence merely because it does not fit yesterday's computational types.**

---

### PIRU: Formal Engineering

PIRU introduces discipline.

It asks for:

```text
clear boundaries
stable identity
explicit interfaces
testability
repeatability
governance
controlled composition
versioning
runtime safety
```

Its bias is:

> **If intelligence is to be reused reliably, its operational contract must eventually become explicit.**

The productive system requires both.

## 10. Emergence and Unitization

The PIRP/PIRU relationship suggests another useful pair of concepts:

> **Emergence <-> Unitization**

Emergence produces intelligence structures whose final computational identity may not yet be known.

Unitization makes sufficiently understood structures reusable.

Conceptually:

```text
Emergence
    |
    v
  PIRP
    |
    v
Unitization
    |
    v
  PIRU
    |
    v
Runtime Use
    |
    v
Interaction
    |
    v
New Emergence
```

This is not unlike the broader Structural Intelligence relationship between folding and unfolding.

However, the two pairs describe different dimensions.

### Folding / Unfolding

Concerned primarily with:

> How intelligence structure is compressed, stored, reconstructed, localized, or expanded.

### Emergence / Unitization

Concerned primarily with:

> How newly encountered intelligence becomes a recognizable and engineerable runtime object.

The two cycles can interact.

## 14. Situated Intelligence

The PIRP perspective also highlights **situatedness**.

A formal PIRU may be reused across many contexts.

But once instantiated into a particular runtime environment, its actual intelligence may depend on:

* local state,
* surrounding CallingGraph,
* policy,
* historical context,
* available evidence,
* neighboring PIRPs,
* tools,
* runtime constraints.

Therefore:

```text
Same PIRU
   |
   +----> Context A ----> PIRP-A
   |
   +----> Context B ----> PIRP-B
   |
   +----> Context C ----> PIRP-C
```

The runtime intelligence pieces may behave differently even though they originate from the same formal unit.

This suggests:

> **PIRU captures reusable intelligence structure; PIRP can capture situated runtime intelligence.**

This distinction may become important for localization and growth.

Suppose:

Are these the same intelligence piece?

There is no universal answer yet.

Possible identity criteria may involve:

* origin,
* purpose,
* structural continuity,
* interface continuity,
* behavioral continuity,
* lineage,
* context,

This suggests that PIRP identity may itself be structural rather than merely nominal.

```text
same instance

same family
same interface

```

These are different notions of sameness.

PIRP/PIRU therefore opens an identity problem that conventional object identity alone may not solve.

## 16. The Danger of Premature Unitization

Formalization is necessary, but it carries a cost.

Suppose an emerging PIRP contains:

```text
A
+
B
+
C
+
unknown relation X
```

An engineer may recognize only:

```text
A
+
B
```

and define a PIRU accordingly.

The remaining structure:

```text
C
+
X
```

may be discarded as noise.

But later evidence may show that X was precisely the mechanism responsible for useful intelligence.

Therefore a growth-capable system should preserve some relationship between:

```text
Original PIRP
      |
      v
Formalized PIRU
      |
      v
Leftover / Unresolved Structure
```

This suggests an important design principle:

> **Unitization should not silently erase unexplained structure.**

Unexplained leftovers may contain future growth opportunities.

This connects naturally to explicit leftover handling in structural runtime systems.

## 17. Counter-Evidence Protects Against Over-Formalization

Counter-evidence becomes particularly important during PIRP-to-PIRU conversion.

Suppose a candidate PIRU claims:

```text
PIRP-X
   |
   v
Formal Interpretation Y
```

The system should also search for:

```text
Evidence supporting Y
        +
Evidence contradicting Y
        +
Contexts where Y fails
```

This can prevent premature closure.

The process becomes:

```text
PIRP
  |
  v
Candidate Formalization
  |
  +----> Supporting Evidence
  |
  +----> Counter-Evidence
  |
  v
Revised Boundary
  |
  v
PIRU
```

Thus PIRU formation can itself be treated as a structural search and validation process.

## 18. Growth Requires Returning to the Pre-Categorical Layer

A purely formal system tends to assume that future intelligence must fit existing units.

Computational Growth Intelligence requires the opposite possibility:

> **New runtime behavior may create structures for which no existing unit is adequate.**

Therefore growth requires a return path:

```text
Known PIRUs
    |
    v
Runtime Composition
    |
    v
Unexpected Structural Delta
    |
    v
New PIRP
    |
    v
New Investigation
```

This is why PIRP remains necessary even in a mature PIRU ecosystem.

Without PIRP, the system risks becoming structurally closed.

With PIRP, the system can preserve novelty until a new formalization is discovered.

### Category-First

* behavior is stable,
* safety requires strong contracts.

### Intelligence-First

Useful when:

* the domain is exploratory,
* structures are emerging,

## 21. A First-Principles View of Runtime Intelligence

The PIRP/PIRU framework encourages a simple methodological reset.

Instead of beginning with:

> Which software abstraction should represent this?

begin with:

> **What intelligence is actually present?**

Then ask:

1. What does it observe?
2. What context does it require?
3. What does it remember?
4. What can it do?
5. What structure does it carry?
6. What can trigger it?
7. What can it trigger?
8. What evidence supports its behavior?
9. How can it change?
10. What remains unexplained?
11. Can it be transferred?
12. Can another intelligence continue it?

Only then ask:

> What should its formal computational representation be?

This sequence places ontology before convenience without rejecting engineering discipline.

## 24. Core Principles

The discussion can be condensed into several working principles.

### Principle 1 — Intelligence Precedes Classification

> **Intelligence may exist before we know which computational category best describes it.**

### Principle 2 — Preserve Before Forcing

> **Do not discard emerging intelligence merely because it does not fit existing computational types.**

### Principle 3 — PIRP Before PIRU

> **Preserve and understand the runtime piece before demanding complete unitization.**

This is a methodological principle, not a universal execution order.

### Principle 4 — Formalization Is Necessary but Provisional

> **A PIRU is an operational stabilization of intelligence, not necessarily its final form.**

### Principle 5 — Runtime Can Reopen Formal Structure

> **Execution, interaction, and growth may transform a PIRU into new PIRPs requiring new formalization.**

### Principle 6 — Leftovers Are Potential Intelligence

> **Unexplained structure should be preserved explicitly when possible rather than silently discarded.**

### Principle 7 — Multiple Perspectives May Coexist

> **Function, state, CCC, DNA, CallingGraph, policy, and other abstractions may describe different projections of the same runtime intelligence.**

### Principle 8 — Growth Requires an Open Ontology

> **A computational growth system must permit meaningful structures that do not yet belong to established categories.**

## 26. Conclusion

The distinction between PIRP and PIRU begins with a simple observation:

> **The intelligence we encounter may be richer than the categories through which we currently describe it.**

Traditional computational abstractions remain indispensable.

But Structural Intelligence and Computational Growth Intelligence increasingly require a layer before those abstractions become fixed.

PIRP provides that layer.

It preserves runtime intelligence as encountered:

* partially bounded,
* situated,
* heterogeneous,
* potentially novel,
* and capable of further growth.

PIRU provides the complementary engineering layer.

It turns sufficiently understood intelligence into something:

* bounded,
* typed,
* testable,
* observable,
* composable,
* reusable,
* and governable.

The resulting duality is:

```text
PIRP
 |
 | discover
 | understand
 | bound
 | validate
 v
PIRU
 |
 | execute
 | compose
 | localize
 | reuse
 v
Runtime
 |
 | interact
 | encounter novelty
 | grow
 v
New PIRP
```

The larger principle is:

> **Intelligence precedes its computational classification.**

And the corresponding engineering principle is:

> **Preserve the runtime intelligence piece first; understand and unitize it second.**

This creates a bridge between two requirements that are often treated as opposites:

> **open-ended intelligence growth**
> and
> **disciplined computational engineering**.

PIRP protects the former.

PIRU enables the latter.

Their continuous interaction may provide one of the structural foundations for intelligence systems that can not only execute known computation, but also discover, preserve, formalize, and grow new computational intelligence.
