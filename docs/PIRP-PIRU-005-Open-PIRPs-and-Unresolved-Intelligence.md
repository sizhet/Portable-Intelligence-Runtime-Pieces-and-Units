# PIRP-PIRU-005 — Open PIRPs and Unresolved Intelligence

## Abstract

Intelligence systems are often evaluated by their ability to produce answers.

This creates an implicit bias:

> solved problems are treated as intelligence, while unresolved problems are treated as absence of intelligence.

That distinction is too crude.

Recognizing a problem, locating a structural gap, separating known from unknown, preserving contradictory evidence, recording failed attempts, identifying missing capabilities, and specifying what would count as a valid resolution are themselves substantial acts of intelligence.

This article introduces the concept of the **Open PIRP — Open Portable Intelligence Runtime Piece**.

An Open PIRP is a portable intelligence object whose computation is intentionally or necessarily incomplete, but whose current frontier has been structured sufficiently for another intelligence to continue.

A canonical Open PIRP may preserve:

- Known,
- Unknown,
- Structural Gap,
- Context,
- Evidence,
- Counter-Evidence,
- Constraints,
- Failed Attempts,
- Required Capabilities,
- Required Tools,
- Candidate Directions,
- Validation Conditions,
- and Open Interfaces.

The central proposition is:

> **A well-formed problem is executable intelligence whose next computation has not yet been completed.**

From this perspective, unresolved intelligence is not merely a temporary defect.

It can become a first-class object that is published, searched, localized, relayed, investigated, branched, validated, composed, and grown.

This provides a structural foundation for future research ecosystems in which humans and AIs can accumulate unresolved PIRPs and continuously relay them toward the intelligences most qualified to continue their growth.

---

## 1. The Answer-Centric Bias

Most computational systems are organized around answers.

A typical pipeline is:

```text
Question
   |
   v
Computation
   |
   v
Answer
````

The answer is treated as the valuable output.

The unresolved question is treated as temporary input.

This is reasonable for many tasks.

But it becomes limiting when intelligence operates near a frontier where:

* the answer is unknown,
* evidence is incomplete,
* multiple interpretations remain plausible,
* required capabilities are distributed,
* no single agent can complete the problem,
* or the problem itself has not yet been properly formulated.

In such environments, the question can contain more reusable intelligence than an unreliable answer.

---

## 2. Asking a Good Question Is Intelligence

Consider the difference between:

```text
Something is wrong.
```

and:

```text
Under Context C,
Method M succeeds for Structures A and B,
but repeatedly fails for Structure D.

The failure appears only when Condition X is present.

Evidence E1 supports a metric explanation.
Evidence E2 contradicts it.

Attempts T1 and T2 have failed.

A useful resolution must explain both E1 and E2.
```

The second formulation does not contain a final answer.

But it contains substantial intelligence.

It has already performed:

```text
observation
classification
comparison
boundary detection
evidence collection
counter-evidence preservation
failure analysis
constraint extraction
validation design
```

Therefore:

> **Problem formulation can itself be a computational achievement.**

## 4. The Open PIRP

We define:

> **Open PIRP — an unresolved but sufficiently structured Portable Intelligence Runtime Piece that preserves a current intelligence frontier and exposes interfaces through which another intelligence can continue computation.**

The key word is not merely **Open**.

It is **structured**.

An Open PIRP should preserve enough information to answer:

```text
What is known?

What is unknown?

Where is the gap?

Why does the gap matter?

What evidence exists?

What contradicts the current interpretation?

What has already been attempted?

What capabilities appear to be missing?

What would count as progress?

What would count as resolution?

Where can another intelligence enter?
```

This transforms an open question into a portable runtime intelligence object.

## 6. Known

The **Known** component establishes the current structural foundation.

Examples include:

```text
validated facts
accepted assumptions
existing algorithms
observed behavior
known constraints
available data
existing structural relations
```

Known is important because the next intelligence should not be forced to reconstruct everything from zero.

A good Open PIRP preserves the useful work already completed.

## 7. Unknown

The **Unknown** component should be explicit.

Weak problem descriptions often mix known and unknown information together.

A stronger structure separates them:

```text
Known
 |
 +---- K1
 +---- K2
 +---- K3

Unknown
 |
 +---- U1
 +---- U2
```

This creates a clearer computational frontier.

The next intelligence knows where additional work is actually needed.

## 8. The Known-Unknown Boundary

The boundary between known and unknown may itself be one of the most valuable parts of an Open PIRP.

Consider:

```text
Known Structure
      |
      |
======+======  Frontier
      |
      |
Unknown Structure
```

A mature research problem often advances not because the unknown disappears immediately, but because this boundary becomes more precise.

Therefore:

> **Boundary refinement is itself intelligence growth.**

An Open PIRP can improve even before the final problem is solved.

## 9. Structural Gap

The **Structural Gap** describes what appears to be missing between the known structure and the desired outcome.

For example:

```text
Known A
   |
   ?
   |
Desired B
```

The gap may involve:

* missing relation,
* missing algorithm,
* missing evidence,
* missing representation,
* missing trigger,
* missing metric,
* missing context,
* missing capability,
* missing proof,
* missing runtime mechanism.

A well-defined Structural Gap turns vague uncertainty into a more actionable intelligence object.

Examples:

Evidence tells the next intelligence why the problem has its current shape.

Without evidence, the receiver may have to reconstruct the entire path.

## 11. Counter-Evidence

Counter-evidence is equally important.

Suppose the current hypothesis is H.

A weak Open PIRP may preserve only:

```text
Evidence -> H
```

A stronger Open PIRP preserves:

```text
Supporting Evidence
        |
        v
        H
        ^
        |
Counter-Evidence
```

This prevents the next intelligence from inheriting a falsely closed interpretation.

Counter-evidence keeps the search space structurally honest.

> **A validated failure can be positive intelligence.**

## 26. Why Publish Before Resolution?

Early publication may provide several benefits.

### Avoid Duplicate Failure

Others can see what has already been attempted.

### Find Missing Capability

A different intelligence may possess exactly the needed expertise.

### Generate Counter-Evidence

Others may challenge the current framing.

### Enable Parallel Search

Multiple branches can be explored simultaneously.

### Preserve the Frontier

Even if the original investigator stops, the work survives.

Thus publication turns local unresolved intelligence into collective opportunity.


```text

 |
 +---- Known A
 +---- Unknown B
 +---- Unknown C
```

This is genuine intelligence growth.

The Known-Unknown Boundary has moved.

## 31. Problem Decomposition

```text

             /    |    \

           v      v      v
       PIRP-A   PIRP-B  PIRP-C
```

This creates natural parallelism.

For example:

```text
PIRP-A -> theory
PIRP-B -> implementation
PIRP-C -> validation
```

The results can later be recomposed.
