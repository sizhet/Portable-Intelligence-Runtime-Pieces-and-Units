# PIRP-PIRU-007 — Relay Localization and Computational Growth Dispatch

## Abstract

Localization is a central operation of Structural Intelligence.

Given a runtime context, a structural search system may locate:

- a relevant node,
- a CCC,
- a CallingPath,
- a PIRU,
- a policy,
- a piece of evidence,
- or another reusable intelligence structure.

This answers an important question:

> **Where is the relevant intelligence?**

Portable Intelligence introduces a second localization problem.

When a PIRP remains unresolved or capable of further growth, the system may need to locate not an existing answer, but the human, AI, algorithm, tool, team, runtime unit, or combination of capabilities most qualified to continue that intelligence.

This asks:

> **Who or what should continue this intelligence?**

This article defines the distinction between:

- **Structural Localization**
- **Relay Localization**

and proposes a broader concept:

> **Computational Growth Localization**

Rather than merely retrieving known intelligence, Computational Growth Localization attempts to place a PIRP into a configuration where productive Structural Delta is likely to occur.

The joint problem can be represented conceptually as:

> **PIRP + Knowledge + Agent + Tool + Context + Policy -> Expected Structural Growth**

This leads to the **PIRP Dispatch Plane**: a structural runtime responsible for matching portable intelligence pieces with the knowledge, capabilities, tools, contexts, and governance conditions required for their productive continuation.

The central proposition is:

> **Localization is not merely finding where an answer already exists. It is finding where a piece of intelligence can most productively continue to exist and grow.**

# Part I — Two Forms of Localization

## 1. Traditional Localization

Structural Intelligence frequently faces a localization problem.

Given:

```text

````

find:

```text

```


or:

```text id="pjpmb1"

     |
     v
Localization
     |
     v
CallingPath
```

The objective is to find an intelligence structure that already exists.

We call this:

> **Structural Localization**

## 2. Structural Localization

Structural Localization asks:

> **Where is the relevant intelligence?**

Possible targets include:

```text id="h7o9xa"

```

The general form is:

```text id="93muwq"

       |
       v

       |
       v

       |
       v

```

This is essential for reusing folded intelligence.

---

## 3. The Second Localization Problem

Now consider an Open PIRP.

```text id="9fyomv"
Open PIRP-X
 |
 +---- Known
 +---- Unknown
 +---- Structural Gap
 +---- Evidence
 +---- Failed Attempts
 +---- Required Capability
```

Suppose no existing PIRU directly resolves it.

Traditional retrieval asks:

> Where is the answer?

But the answer may not yet exist.

A different question is required:

> **Where should this PIRP go next?**

More precisely:

> **Who or what is structurally qualified to continue it?**

This is Relay Localization.

---

## 4. Relay Localization

We define:

> **Relay Localization — the process of locating a human, AI, algorithm, tool, team, PIRU, runtime, or composite intelligence configuration capable of productively continuing a PIRP.**

The target is not necessarily someone who already knows the answer.

The target may instead possess:

* relevant capability,
* useful perspective,
* appropriate tools,
* analogous experience,
* complementary knowledge,
* or an environment in which the PIRP can continue growing.

This distinction is fundamental.

---

## 5. Structural Localization vs Relay Localization

The two problems can be summarized as:

| Localization Type       | Primary Question                               |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Structural Localization | Where is the relevant intelligence?            |
| Relay Localization      | Who or what should continue this intelligence? |

Or:

```text id="e2mpge"
Structural Localization
        |
        v
Find Existing Intelligence

Relay Localization
        |
        v
Find Next Intelligence
```

The first retrieves.

The second dispatches toward growth.

# Part II — Beyond Social Graphs

## 6. The Conventional Human Relay Model

Human collaboration often relies on social structure.

A person encounters a problem and asks:

```text id="rdx5ig"

```

```text id="6px0ku"

   |
   +---- Friend-B
   |
   +---- Colleague-C
   |
   +---- Organization-D
```

This works surprisingly well.

But it is structurally limited.

The most qualified next intelligence may be outside the source agent's social graph.

---

## 7. Social Proximity Is Not Intelligence Proximity

Consider:

```text id="lfkyfk"

 |
 +---- socially close -> Agent-B
```

Yet the PIRP may require a capability possessed by Agent-Z:

```text id="fuc4wl"

 |

 |
 +---- structurally qualified
```

Therefore:

> **Social Proximity is not Intelligence Proximity.**

A PIRP ecosystem should be able to search beyond known social relations.

```text id="4e4qxy"

different software ecosystems
```

This suggests an important target:

> **organizationally distant but structurally near intelligence**

Relay Localization should make such relationships discoverable.

# Part IV — Three Structural Distances

## 15. Knowledge Distance

The first distance asks:

> **Which existing intelligence is structurally near this PIRP?**

Call this:

> **Knowledge Distance**

It may consider:

```text id="fjikcp"
conceptual similarity
structural similarity
shared constraints
shared failure patterns
shared context
shared outcomes
```

This supports Structural Localization.

---

## 16. Capability Distance

The second asks:

> **Which intelligence is structurally qualified to continue this PIRP?**

Call this:

> **Capability Distance**

It may compare:

```text id="g7ixqp"
Required Capability
        vs
Available Capability
```

The best candidate may not be the closest by domain label.

---

## 17. Relay Distance

The third asks:

> **How difficult is it to transfer this PIRP so that the receiver can actually continue it?**

Call this:

> **Relay Distance**

Factors may include:

```text id="ygvgyr"
terminology mismatch
context reconstruction
tool incompatibility
missing background
interface mismatch
policy restrictions
communication cost
validation cost
```

Thus a theoretically capable agent may still have high Relay Distance.

---

A productive relay may require all three distances to be acceptable.
